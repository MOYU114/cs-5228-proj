## Proposed related-work paragraph

Dynamic hedonic models relate housing prices to property attributes while allowing these relationships to vary across time and municipalities [Meese and Wallace]. Tabular-learning benchmarks find that tree methods remain competitive on medium-sized datasets, motivating CatBoost as a categorical-feature baseline [Grinsztajn et al.; Prokhorenkova et al.]. Neural alternatives pursue different mechanisms: TabM uses parameter-efficient MLP ensembles [Gorishniy et al.], while TabPFN learns from synthetic tasks and predicts with labeled examples as context [Hollmann et al.]. ModernNCA instead learns representations for neighborhood-based prediction [Ye et al.]. These approaches complement field-token attention, but they were not run in this study. Our comparison focuses on the reported baselines and BERT under the available future-month protocols.

The Ridge citation fits beside its definition in the baseline paragraph rather than expanding the related-work paragraph. Any implementation claims remain grounded in the project code, not in these papers.
